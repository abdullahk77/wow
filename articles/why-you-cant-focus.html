Why You Can't Focus

6 Min Read • Part 2: Focus

You sit down to work.

You have the time.
You have the plan.
You know exactly what needs to be done.

But within five minutes, you feel a quiet tension.

Your brain is looking for an exit.
And almost automatically, you check your phone.

The Stimulation Trap

Most people blame themselves for this.

They think they lack the willpower to focus.

But willpower has very little to do with it.

Focus isn’t a discipline problem.
It’s a sensitivity problem.

How Your Baseline Shifts

Your brain adapts to whatever you give it.

If you constantly feed it high-stimulation inputs—like short videos, notifications, and endless scrolling—your brain recalibrates.

It sets a new baseline for what feels "normal."

The problem?

Meaningful work is inherently low-stimulation.

Why Deep Work Feels Difficult

Reading a book, writing a plan, or doing deep thinking doesn’t give you immediate spikes of dopamine.

So, compared to your phone, deep work feels physically uncomfortable.

It feels boring.
It feels slow.

You don't lack focus.
Your brain is just rejecting low-stimulation tasks.

How to Reclaim It

You can’t force focus through sheer effort.

If you are fighting a high-stimulation baseline, you will lose.

Instead, you have to reset your sensitivity.

  • Remove the easy exits.
  • Embrace brief moments of boredom.
  • Let your brain adjust to slower inputs.

Once your baseline drops, focus returns naturally.
